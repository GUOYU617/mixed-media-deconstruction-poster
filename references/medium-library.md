# Medium Library

Assign media by the visual job each registered source region must perform. Keep every region at its original coordinates, scale, contour, viewpoint, and adjacency.

## In-place application rules

- Apply marks inside or directly across the source region they replace. Never move the treated subject to a clearer area.
- Match important edges and intersections before adding texture.
- Continue contours across photo, watercolor, graphite, line, and crayon boundaries at the same coordinate and angle.
- Let low-priority detail thin out into incomplete strokes or bare paper; do not replace it with a relocated close-up.
- Use one original-photo region when needed for identity or factual detail. The hand-made treatment must meet that photo region without duplicating its contents.
- Sample colors from the source photograph. Translate pigment, pencil, and crayon using those local hues instead of applying a global vintage palette.

## Watercolor

Use for atmosphere, translucent mass, light, vegetation, sky, water, soft material, or large color fields.

Pixel cues: layered transparent washes, wet-on-wet blooms, granulation, soft pooling edges, uneven pigment density, reserved paper highlights, no glossy digital gradient.

Avoid using watercolor for tiny mechanical details or critical geometry unless reinforced by line work.

## Graphite sketch

Use for proportion, construction, volume, joints, perspective, facade rhythm, folds, and structural transitions.

Pixel cues: HB–4B line variation, searching construction lines, cross-hatching, smudged graphite, kneaded-eraser highlights, visible paper tooth.

Preserve key axes and counts. Avoid polished photoreal pencil rendering that erases the sketch process.

## Simple line drawing

Use for iconic outline, labels, repeated modules, interfaces, connections, and relational diagrams.

Pixel cues: economical black or colored-ink contour, open shapes, variable hand pressure, occasional broken line, almost no shading, clear silhouette at thumbnail scale.

Keep it hand-drawn, not vector-perfect, clip-art-like, or childish unless requested.

## Crayon or oil pastel

Use for tactile surfaces, foliage, fabric, energetic accents, repeated color rhythms, ground, or emotionally warm parts.

Pixel cues: wax drag, broken coverage, blunt strokes, layered cross-direction marks, visible paper gaps, pressure variation, crumb-like edge texture.

Limit the crayon area when the source has delicate identity details. Avoid rainbow classroom-poster drift.

## Optional supporting media

- **Ink wash:** decisive dark mass, shadow, gesture, calligraphic movement.
- **Dry brush:** rough stone, bark, weathered surfaces, motion traces.
- **Colored pencil:** controlled color detail and fine texture between graphite and crayon.
- **Paper cutout:** clear massing, overlap, framing, and large structural color, but only as an in-place registered paper layer. Never translate, rotate, independently scale, detach, or float the fragment.
- **Halftone or xerox:** photo anchor integration and archival reproduction.

Use optional media only when they clarify the part map. Do not add more than five primary media to one poster.

## Default assignment logic

When four suitable units exist, start here and adapt:

| Visual job | Default medium | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| atmosphere or broad mass | watercolor | shows light and volume without hard enclosure |
| load-bearing or proportional structure | graphite | exposes construction and depth |
| outline, opening, connection, or label | simple line drawing | preserves clarity and count |
| tactile surface, movement, or accent | crayon | adds physical color and energy |

## Coherence rules

- Repeat one source-derived accent hue across two or three media in different densities.
- Share one neutral or source-matched pale paper color and subtle scan texture across the entire poster.
- Keep every region in the same source scale and viewing angle. Do not use detail enlargements by default.
- Keep medium boundaries visible but spatially registered, never boxed into separate samples.
- Describe physical marks, not only medium names. “Watercolor” without pigment behavior is too weak.
- Preserve natural white balance and recognizable local colors. Avoid sepia, yellow cream overlays, tobacco brown, orange haze, faded-film grading, heavy desaturation, artificial patina, stains, and burn marks unless explicitly requested.
- Keep at least one clean neutral in the image so source colors remain fresh. Paper texture must not act as a yellow color filter.
