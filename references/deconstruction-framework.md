# Registered Zone Framework

Analyze the source as one coordinate-locked image field. A zone is a region to be treated in place, not an asset to cut out and move.

## Establish the coordinate system

Record:

- source width, height, ratio, and orientation;
- horizon, center axis, dominant diagonal, or pose axis;
- vanishing directions and long continuation lines;
- primary silhouette and important negative shapes;
- overlap order and contact points;
- fixed intersections such as hand-to-object, wheel-to-road, roof-to-wall, face-to-hair, or branch-to-trunk.
- dominant natural hues, neutral references, material colors, light direction, and any local color that identifies the scene;
- story evidence: people, interactions, worn surfaces, ritual or daily-use traces, unusual light, weather, culturally specific detail, or a meaningful relation between subject and setting;
- candidate tear paths already suggested by tree crowns, branches, rooflines, eaves, ridges, shorelines, roads, shadows, garments, or figure silhouettes.

Describe approximate positions as source-relative regions such as `upper-left 0%–35% width / 10%–45% height`. Percentages are planning aids; do not turn them into visible boxes.

## Assign priority

- **A — preserve:** identity, pose, essential geometry, key object, defining texture, readable sign when requested, or a story-rich photographic anchor. Prefer real details that explain where, who, what happened, or how the place is lived.
- **B — translate:** supporting structures that may become watercolor, graphite, line drawing, crayon, ink, or paper texture while staying registered.
- **C — fade/omit:** visually noisy or low-information sky, ground, shadow, distant clutter, repeated texture, or peripheral detail that may dissolve into paper.

Never assign identity-bearing faces, hands in contact, product controls, major architectural openings, wheels, structural joints, or essential tools to C unless the user explicitly requests omission.

## Build the zone map

Choose 3–7 non-overlapping or softly overlapping regions that follow real visual boundaries. Keep enough coverage to reconstruct the original composition mentally.

```text
Zone: <name>
Source location: <relative coordinates or named region>
Priority: <A / B / C>
Visible evidence: <source fact>
Locked geometry: <position, contour, count, intersection, or line continuation>
Treatment: <photo / watercolor / graphite / line / crayon / fade to paper>
Boundary behavior: <torn edge / dry fade / pigment bleed / seamless overlap>
Boundary source: <named tree / roof / shoreline / road / silhouette / soft low-detail field>
Confidence: <high / medium / low>
```

## Domain anchors

### Scene or architecture

Lock horizon, building footprints, rooflines, facade openings, road edges, rails, poles, wires, vehicles, people, and their vanishing directions. Fade sky, ground texture, distant windows, or peripheral clutter before moving anything.

### Product or object

Lock silhouette, proportions, controls, seams, joints, ports, handles, and contact with the ground. Fade reflections or background clutter before simplifying required features.

### Plant or animal

Lock overall posture, main axis, limb or branch count, joints, markings, and ground contact. Fade dense repeated foliage or background texture before altering anatomy.

### Person or artwork

Lock identity, face position, body proportions, pose, hands, held objects, garment silhouette, and gaze direction. Keep the body continuous; change the surface medium in place.

## Continuity rules

- Continue a line through a media boundary at the same angle and coordinate.
- Let the photographic and drawn versions meet once; do not show both versions in different places.
- Keep part size tied to source perspective. No independent enlargement or rotation.
- Every generated image still needs 2–4 clearly visible torn-paper seams. If a sensitive boundary is uncertain, reroute the seam along a safer nearby source contour and use a soft transition only across the sensitive feature; soft transitions may supplement but never replace the mandatory seams.
- If the source is already sparse, use fewer media instead of inventing extra zones.
- Do not use straight horizontal or vertical tears merely to divide media. Let hard tears attach to real source contours and let low-information areas open into paper.
- Give every mandatory seam two different declared media. Preserve the same source coordinates on both sides, and show irregular white fibers, a slight lifted lip, paper thickness, and a very shallow shadow without turning the seam into a detached object.
- Preserve story evidence before visually impressive but generic detail. A small real interaction may outrank a large texture field.
